Trump trims Brazil beef, coffee, fruit tariffs by 10pc
Market
:
Agriculture
15/11/25
US president Donald Trump lifted 10pc tariffs on imports of Brazilian beef, coffee and fruits imposed in April, but 40pc tariffs imposed in August and other quota-tied fees remain in effect.
The executive order goes into effect retroactively on "goods entered for consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption" after 12:01am ET on 13 November.
Brazil is a major supplier of these products to the US. Brazil's foreign affairs minister Mauro Vieira and the US secretary of state Marco Rubio
have discussed tariffs in recent weeks
.
Starting in early August, a combination of tariffs equaling 76pc were imposed on US imports of Brazilian beef,
cutting those volumes in half
. Australia currently fills most US needs for beef, which are subject to a 10pc tariff. While Brazil had a 50pc tariff on in-quota shipments and a 76.4pc tariff on out-of-quota shipments, that has now been reduced by 10 percentage points.
US beef imports are forecast at 2.433mn t in 2025, up 16pc from 2024, before easing slightly to 2.245mn t in 2026, according to the US Department of Agriculture. But margins remain tight,
squeezed by the volatile tariffs
and shifting consumer behavior, importers and exporters said.
Tariffs also
reduced shipments of Brazilian coffee
and
orange juice
, other key products exported to the US.
By João Curi

interest in technologies that can support fertilizer-use efficiency while
reducing environmental losses. With limited agricultural land, Singapore is
increasingly pursuing regional partnerships that combine technology, training

Brunei, Bangladesh and other southeast Asian markets. Industry participants said

China's beef quotas reshape Australian trade
Dalby, 28 October (Argus) — China's new beef import quotas are forcing
Australian exporters to redirect record volumes into smaller alternative
markets, with lower prices attracting new buyers, Australian traders told Argus
. The three-year quota system, introduced by China in January, prompted
Australia to front-load beef shipments to China before its 205,000t tariff-free
allocation was filled on 20 June. A 55pc tariff now applies to shipments above
the quota until it resets on 1 January. China took around 37,800t less
Australian beef than a year earlier in January-September, with shipments falling
by 18pc to around 169,700t. Despite this, total Australian beef exports rose by
7pc over the same period, from around 1.127mn t to a record 1.21mn t, as
exporters redirected supply into other markets and expanded their share of
established destinations such as the US. Shipments to the US increased by
51,519t, or 16pc, to around 380,000t. Exports to South Korea rose by 41,547t, or
25pc, to around 206,700t, more than offsetting the decline in shipments to China
and pushing South Korea ahead of Japan as Australia's second-largest beef export
destination in January-September. The shift became more pronounced after China's
safeguard tariff took effect in June. Australian shipments to China fell by 68pc
on the year in July-September, dropping by around 50,800t to 24,248t from
75,079t a year earlier, leaving exporters to find buyers for displaced supplies.
Smaller markets absorbed some of the excess. Australian beef exports to Taiwan
increased by 39pc from July-September 2025, while shipments to Thailand rose by
20pc, Malaysia by 15pc and Canada by 13pc. Combined exports to the four markets
increased by 21pc, from around 36,000t to 43,500t. But redirecting the large
volumes traditionally sold into China at comparable prices proved difficult,
traders said. Uncertainty over when the quota would be filled contributed to
Australian fattier beef trim prices falling from mid-April as Chinese buying
slowed sharply. Australian 65pc chemical lean (65cl) frozen boxed beef trim fell
by A$1.44/kg from 10 April to 7 August, when Chinese buying resumed for
shipments scheduled to arrive after the quota reset. Buying interest from
southeast and east Asian importers increased around the quota trigger as lower
prices made Australian fattier trim more attractive. Large-volume Australian
65cl trades to Taiwan and the Philippines were reported at A$6.10/kg as buyers
took advantage of the lower market. Fattier trim prices subsequently
strengthened as Chinese buying returned from August. By late October, however,
trades of Australian HGP-free fattier trim to China had again become limited,
with many importers staying out of the market while assessing the large volumes
expected to arrive early next year. Some market participants are concerned that
the simultaneous arrival of substantial Australian and South American beef
volumes following the tariff-rate quota reset could create logistical
bottlenecks in China early next year. By Alys Marshall Australian beef export

Bolsonaro to challenge Brazil election results
Sao Paulo, 26 October (Argus) — Brazilian senator Flavio Bolsonaro said it will
be "impossible" to accept the results of his Sunday loss to incumbent president
Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, and that he would seek to have the election annulled.
Lula beat Bolsonaro in the runoff on 25 October with nearly 51.8pc of votes. The
supreme electoral court (TSE) extended voting by two hours following requests by
both Lula and Bolsonaro's parties. Bolsonaro later called that decision
"unacceptable" and said that he would seek the election's annulment. Most Latin
American leaders have congratulated Lula on his victory. The exception is
Argentina's Javier Milei. US president Donald Trump has also yet to acknowledge
Brazil's electoral results. Showing up Lula's win came on the back of historic
voter turnout in Brazil. During the first round on 4 October — which Bolsonaro
led with 47pc of votes' against Lula's 45.16pc — over 33mn Brazilians, or nearly
21.1pc of voters, did not vote. That figure fell to 30.5mn in the second round —
or 19.2pc of voters — the lowest in 20 years. By Lucas Parolin Send comments and

Lula completes comeback, wins Brazil elections
Sao Paulo, 25 October (Argus) — Incumbent Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva will serve
as Brazil's president for another four-year term, after beating senator Flavio
Bolsonaro in the presidential runoff. With nearly 100pc of votes counted, Lula
led Bolsonaro with over 51.77pc of votes. Lula becomes the first in Brazil's
history to win a presidential election after trailing in the first round of
voting. He received 45.16pc of votes on 4 October, trailing Bolsonaro's 47pc.
This will be Lula's fourth stint as Brazil's president. He first served two
terms in 2003-2011. Lula's campaign focused on social media following his
first-round defeat. Street demonstrations, especially in Sao Paulo and Rio de
Janeiro — Brazil's most populous cities — may have also given him a boost. The
final televised debate, held on 23 October, may have also helped him swing
votes. Fact-checking agencies showed him saying more truthful statements than
Bolsonaro, which may have weakened his adversary's campaign. The second round of
voting saw fewer absentee voters as well. Around 21pc of voters abstained in the
first round, versus around 19pc in the second round. Around 3mn people
additional people showed up to vote compared with the first round, and Lula
obtained 10mn votes more than in the first round. Voting delays Voting booths
stayed open two hours later than usual after both Lula and Bolsonaro's parties
requested an extension. Lula's party claimed that problems in public transport
denied voters the chance to vote. The federal police arrested Pablo Souza, the
mobility secretary of Salvador — the capital of Bahia state, historically a
left-leaning state — for reducing the amount of buses available on Sunday.
Bolsonaro's party requested the extension in voting hours because of rains in
Minas Gerais and Rio Grande do Sul states. Flavio Bolsonaro's brother, Eduardo
Bolsonaro — a former congressman who has been in the US since March 2025 and was
convicted by the supreme court of coercion with the US to interfere in his
father's trial — said before the elections that the candidate Bolsonaro would
also head to the US if defeated in the elections. It is still unclear whether
Flavio Bolsonaro will remain in Brazil or not. The candidate had yet to concede
defeat as of 8:30pm ET. Backing Petrobras During his 2023-2026 term, Lula put
state-controlled Petrobras at the center of his energy policy, seeking to
broaden the firm's scope. He ended the company's import price parity principle —
in a bid to control fuel prices — and his predecessor's policy of selling
assets. He returned Petrobras to markets such as fertilizer production . He also
spoke in favor of increasing oil and gas production, while also seeking to put
it in the center of Brazil's energy transition. Deforestation also fell during
his term , after Lula reversed policies of his predecessor, Jair Bolsonaro —
Flavio's father, who is serving a 27-year sentence for attempting to overturn
the result of the 2022 election. Bolsonaro would have likely sought to win
amnesty for his father, but that is unlikely to take place now that Lula will
remain as president. Lula will also be responsible for picking four or five
supreme court judges in the next four years. One seat is vacant, three others
will retire by 2030 and judge Alexandre de Moraes could face impeachment
pressure from the right-leaning congress. Some of Lula's main campaign promises
dealt with the economy, including maintaining the current fiscal framework while
preserving room for public investment, expanding infrastructure spending,
advancing the government's industrial policy and mobilizing investment for
transitions to cleaner energy. Adversaries heavily criticized his fiscal
framework, as Brazil's debt — including the federal, state and municipal
governments — reached R11.1 trillion ($2.2 trillion) in August, up from R7.2
trillion at the end of 2022. Lula also promises to continue increasing the
minimum wage, expand credit access for small- and medium-sized businesses,
strengthen protections for app-based gig workers and pursue a more progressive
tax system in which higher-income individuals contribute a larger share of
taxes. But the heavily right-wing congress will probably be a hurdle for Lula to
enact many of those promises. Lula's win puts Brazil as one of the sole
countries in Latin America with a left-wing federal government, after Argentina,
Peru, Chile, Colombia and others have elected right-wing presidents recently.
Lula's relationship with the US and President Donald Trump were rocky at times ,
especially because of the tariffs imposed by the US president. Lula has been
especially stern with the US' interest in Brazil's critical minerals. By Lucas
