Earlier this week, U.S. President Donald Trump announced his intention to slap 50 per cent tariffs on some Canadian goods, including alcohol and dairy, escalating the trade war between the longtime allies.
The tariffs are slated to take effect on Aug. 19.
The move is in response to retaliatory tariffs imposed on the U.S. by Canada following Trump's previous tariffs.
Tariffs have been making headlines on and off since Trump first took office, and it can get confusing. Here's a brief timeline that shows how Trump's trade offensive has unfolded in Canada.

Starting Aug. 29, the U.S. will put an end to the exemption that allows Canadian retailers to ship products to the U.S. duty-free. It's called the "de minimis exemption," and it allows goods under $800 to enter the U.S. without duties. The exemption has been in place for nearly a century, with the threshold increasing to 800 under former U.S. president Barack Obama. U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order last month to remove it.
Carney says Trump reassured him during a call that such a move will help the two countries launch new trade negotiations.
This month also sees the end of the "de minimis" rule, which means low-value imports coming into the U.S. lose their duty-free status. Goods from Canada are impacted, and
mom and pop shops are hit particularly hard.
