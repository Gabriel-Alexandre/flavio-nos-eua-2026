Operation Southern Spear
is a United States military and surveillance campaign launched in 2025 by the
administration
of US president
Donald Trump
with the declared aims of "detecting, disrupting, and degrading transnational criminal and illicit maritime networks".
[
56
]
Some analysts say the operation is a hybridization of the
war on terror
and the
war on drugs
. Others have said that
removal
of
Nicolás Maduro
from power in
Venezuela
was an objective of the operation.
In mid-August 2025, the US began
deploying military forces to the Caribbean Sea
, amid
heightened tensions
between the US and Venezuela during the
crisis in Venezuela
and as the Trump administration sought to slow the flow of illicit drugs. In September, the U.S. military began conducting
airstrikes on vessels
the Trump administration alleged were controlled by
narco-terrorists
. By late November, with the
Cartel of the Suns
designated by the US as a
Foreign Terrorist Organization
and a
Specially Designated Global Terrorist
organization, a new phase of operations began. In December, the Trump administration declared the Maduro government a Foreign Terrorist Organization, and began
boarding and seizing sanctioned crude tankers
.
[
57
]
In late December, the US made its
first strike on a land target within Venezuela
. On 3 January 2026, the US
bombed targets in Caracas
and other locations, and captured Maduro and flew him out of the country.
Later in January, NPR reported that "drug-seizure data suggest that the attacks are doing little to stop the flow of illegal narcotics into the United States."
[
58
]
On 6 March 2026, the operation expanded to Ecuador when U.S. forces bombed
Comandos de la Frontera
as part of the
Ecuadorian conflict (2024–present)
.
[
59
]
[
60
]
[
20
]
The campaign was formally named on 13
November 2025 by
Pete Hegseth
, US
secretary of defense
, as an expansion of an operation bearing the same name announced by the
United States Navy
on 28
January 2025. It uses a hybrid fleet of vessels with
robotics
and autonomous systems to detect and combat alleged
drug trafficking
networks in the
Western Hemisphere
.
According to an assessment by the
Drug Enforcement Administration
(DEA), the military strikes against suspected drug-trafficking vessels did not reduce the volume of
cocaine entering the United States
. Instead, the operations forced criminal organizations to change their smuggling techniques and at the same time eroded traditional law enforcement investigative techniques.

stated that some experts believe that the "scope and intensity" of the Operation indicate that the Trump administrations's goals extend beyond combatting drug trafficking to a "broader plan to oust Maduro".
[
132
]
An analysis by the
Costs of War Project
estimated that Operation Southern Spear and
Operation Absolute Resolve
, from 1 August 2025 to 31 March 2026, cost at least $4.7 billion in government funds.
[
133
]
Reactions
[
edit
]
Foreign Policy
magazine stated that a poll of Latin Americans living across the Americas, including the US and Canada, in October 2025 showed "fairly strong levels of support among people in the region for a US military intervention in Venezuela to depose Maduro and his government", adding that people in Latin America were more "likely to support such a scenario" than US respondents.
[
134
]
[
135
]
Following the 3 January 2026 capture of Nicolás Maduro by the United States military, protests occurred in several cities. In Chicago, Washington DC, and Times Square in New York City, people rallied to protest the United States’ intervention in Venezuela, claiming it was “about stealing oil and dominating Latin America.”
[
136
]
Protesters labelled the Trump Administration’s use of military force in Venezuela without Congressional support illegal, saying the attacks were “an act of imperialism that Americans did not vote for.”
[
137
]
The activist group that organized the Times Square protest was the ANSWER Coalition, a national anti-war and anti-racism social movement that called for a cease of US bombings and attacks on Venezuela.
[
136
]
Zoe Alexandra of the ANSWER Coalition stated, "As people of the United States, we have to oppose this action because what's stopping Trump from doing this to other countries?"
[
138
]
At the same time in Queens, New York, a group of people organized in support of the Trump Administration’s actions, celebrating the removal of Nicolás Maduro from office. The public reactions immediately following the events of 3 January 2026 were highly divided—New York Times reports, “the U.S. military action has been polarizing, with large numbers of residents turning out in opposition, and many Venezuelan immigrants taking to the streets to rejoice.”
[
139
]
During Maduro’s 5 January 2026 federal court arraignment, two opposing groups of activists gathered outside the courthouse building.
[
139
]
One group consisted of anti-intervention activists protesting the US’ economic and political involvement in Latin America, while the other group consisted of Venezuelan immigrants and opposers of Maduro’s government who rallied in support of the regime change in Venezuela, chanting “Libertad!” and “It already fell; it already fell; this dictatorship already fell!”
[
139
]
In Venezuela, public opinion about the US’ actions in the War on cartels was also split. In late 2025, at the beginning of the United States’ War on cartels and before the capture of Nicolás Maduro, “Venezuela’s top pollster found that 23% of Venezuelans supported foreign military intervention and 55% opposed it—even as the vast majority of Venezuelans opposed Maduro himself.”
[
140
]
Following the events of 3 January 2026, protests broke out in major cities including Caracas, Barquisimeto, Ciudad Guayana, Maracaibo and Mérida.
[
141
]
In the largest of this series of protests, “Student demonstrators gathered on the campus of the Central University of Venezuela in Caracas on Thursday to demand the release of all of the country’s political prisoners, the return of exiled activists and a full transition to democracy.”
[
141
]
At the same time, many citizens celebrated the country “entering a new, less authoritarian era” following the removal of Maduro from power.
[
141
]
Many social activists who had been arrested or in hiding since the election of Maduro in 2024 participated in these rallies, including Yerwin Torrealba, an activist leader for exiled Nobel laureate María Corina Machado’s movement Vente Venezuela, and Jesús Armas, another prominent opposition leader.
[
141
]
Although some think the volume of protests and increase in social activism in Venezuela after the United States’ capture of Nicolás Maduro point to some degree of change in the country’s authoritarian politics, Maduro’s regime remains largely the same, led by his vice president Delcy Rodríguez, and the extent to which the regime has actually changed is unclear.
