Tau commits to taking action on forced labour imports following higher US tariffs

South Africa “will continue to engage” with the US with a view to “either eliminate or reduce” the 12.5% tariff imposed on SA exports to the US.
Minister of Trade, Industry and Competition Parks Tau said in a statement on Saturday that the tariffs that took effect on Friday came in spite of “numerous written submissions by the South African government, organised labour and South African private sector; and extensive consultations held with the office of the US Trade Representative [USTR] in May 2026; and testimony by the South African government at the public hearing held by the US Section 301 Committee earlier this month.”
Tau said that, at the public hearings in Washington, government had committed to “publishing a notice in the [Government] Gazette requesting public comments on intent to issue regulation to prohibit goods produced using, in whole or in part, forced labour and child labour.”
ADVERTISEMENT
SA was one of 60 countries and regions investigated for not doing enough to prevent the importation of goods manufactured under labour practices that could be classified as “forced labour”. The USTR instituted the investigation when the US Supreme Court ruled that the so-called “reciprocal” trade tariffs instituted in terms of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act was unlawful, causing a change of tack. The act could only be used to address active international crises, the Supreme Court found.
Tau reiterated that many products are exempt from all tariffs, including macadamia nuts, oranges, limes, tea, spices, seeds, cane sugar, orange and lime juice, syrups, chemicals, critical minerals such as platinum-group and precious metals, isotopes, civil aircraft and components, and pharmaceuticals.
Business Unity SA CEO Khulekani Mathe previously told News24 that the 12.5% tariffs have not caused any “new anxieties”, and were significantly better than the 30% that applied from August 2025 to February 2026, until they were struck down.

Agri SA viewed it as good news that several of South Africa’s key agricultural competitor countries are subject to similar tariffs, “helping maintain a more level competitive landscape.”
However, certain agricultural sectors are highly reliant on US exports and may continue to feel pain, affecting jobs and incomes in certain rural areas.
